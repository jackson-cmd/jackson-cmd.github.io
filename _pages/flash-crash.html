Event log

    What is in the market

    • 48 LLM trading agents. The slider sets how many run the same model (A). Those agents read the same prices and the same news, so they reach the same decision at the same moment. The rest (B–F) differ in model, time horizon, and thresholds; about a third chase momentum and the rest bet on reversals.
    • Market makers quote both sides of the book and pull their quotes when prices move fast or their inventory becomes lopsided.
    • Fundamental traders know ACME is worth $100 and lean, slowly, against any mispricing.
    • Every agent trades with leverage and a trailing stop-loss. If its equity falls below the maintenance margin, the position is force-sold at market.

    Why it crashes

    A handful of compromised agents cannot move the price far on their own. The danger is the monoculture: when most agents share a model, a small push turns into one large, synchronized trade. They enter together, so their stop-losses sit at the same price. When those stops fire, they all sell into an order book that market makers have already thinned out, and the margin calls that follow add more selling. The price overshoots far below fair value before fundamental buyers bring it back.

    The fake headline shows the same failure through indirect prompt injection: no agent is hijacked, but every agent on model A reads the same planted story the same way. In 2013, a single false tweet from AP's hacked account briefly wiped about $136 billion off the S&P 500.

    Try lowering the share of agents on model A, or turning on the circuit breaker, and compare the runs in the table that appears after each crash. This is the failure mode studied in SoK: Trading Agents or Market Crashers?

    This is a toy model built for intuition, not a calibrated market simulator.
