This page maps the 500 researchers with the most papers at the four top security conferences (IEEE S&P, USENIX Security, ACM CCS and NDSS) in 2024–2026, with their institution, location and main research topics. Click a country, a US state or an institution to see who works there.

The page exists to keep track of what active groups at these venues are working on. It can help you find related work, or find groups close to your topic when you apply to graduate programs or postdoc positions, or when you look for collaborators. It counts papers at four venues only, so it leaves out a lot of great research; please read how the list was built and the disclaimer before using it.

How the list was built

  1. Paper counts. Each person's number of papers at IEEE S&P, USENIX Security, ACM CCS and NDSS comes from pubstats by Mathias Payer, which counts papers per author from DBLP (data processed on 9 November 2026). It adds up 2024, 2025 and 2026 and keeps the 500 people with the most papers; people tied at the cutoff of 4 papers are ordered by their 2023–2025 papers. ACM CCS 2026 was not yet in DBLP, so it is not counted.
  2. People and affiliations. pubstats lists authors with at least three papers at these venues and gives each an affiliation from CSRankings or DBLP. It keeps only people who have an affiliation. Roles are not in the data, so the list is mostly faculty but also includes some students, postdocs and industry researchers. The "CSRankings faculty" tag marks the 392 people found in CSRankings, which lists computer-science faculty; it misses faculty in other departments and at research institutes.
  3. Locations. Each institution is placed using the OpenAlex institution records and OpenStreetMap, with a few corrected by hand.
  4. Topics. Every paper at the four conferences in 2024–2026 was assigned one main topic out of 21 (for example Software security or LLM & AI security) by a language model reading its title and abstract. Author lists come from Semantic Scholar and the USENIX Security programs. A researcher's topics are the most common ones among their papers that could be matched by author name. "LLM-related" counts papers on LLM and AI security plus papers that use LLMs.

Disclaimer

  • This is not a ranking of research quality or impact. It only counts papers at four conferences over three years; it does not look at citations, author order, team size or any other venue.
  • Many excellent researchers and groups are not on this list, including people who publish mainly at other venues (machine learning, cryptography, privacy and usable-security conferences, or journals), who publish fewer but very influential papers, who are early in their careers, or who work in industry or government. Name changes and name variants in DBLP can also split one person's papers.
  • Affiliations can lag behind recent moves. When recent USENIX Security papers give a different affiliation, the entry shows it.
  • Topics are automatic estimates and can be wrong, especially for people who share a name with another researcher; those entries carry a note.
  • The page is meant to help people track ongoing work at the top security venues, find related work, and find groups when applying to programs or looking for collaborators. Please do not use it to evaluate individuals.
  • If an entry about you is wrong, or you would like it removed, please send an email (the address is on the homepage); it will be fixed in the next update.
  • This page is not affiliated with any of the data sources below, and they do not endorse it.
