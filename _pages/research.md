My research is on **agentic security in high-stakes domains**, where LLM agents act with real authority and a single compromised action can cause irreversible harm.

A modern agent is more than its model: a harness wraps the model with prompts, tools, skills, and memory, and runs an agentic loop that plans, acts, and consumes untrusted inputs at every step. I study attacks across this stack — prompt injection, indirect prompt injection, prompt leaking, jailbreak, and memory poisoning — and defenses that keep agents secure once they are deployed in real systems.

My current work covers three kinds of agents:

- **Rogue AI agents**: replicating reported agent incidents offline to find what caused them and which defenses stop them ([*ROGUE-AI*](/publication/2026-11-rogue-ai)).
- **Trading agents**: information injection attacks through fabricated news ([*Fake News, Real Crashes*](/publication/2026-10-fake-news-real-crashes)) and how academic trading agents fail under market stress and attack ([*Trading Agents or Market Crashers?*](/publication/2026-09-trading-agents-market-crashers)).
- **Options-trading agents**: measuring the safeguards that broker and open-source options agents enforce, and the losses they can reach ([*Unbounded Losses, Unchecked Inputs*](/publication/2026-10-options-trading-agents)).
